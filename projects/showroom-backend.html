Summary

The .NET backend of Spark Digital Showroom. It serves the product tree, renders configured rooms with tiles, wood floors and furniture, and produces the summary, PDF, mail and order a shopper leaves with. The product's features and the decisions that span its parts are on the Spark Digital Showroom page; this page holds the rest of the backend work.

AI-assisted since April 2026: from then on, much of my work here was written with Claude Code. I made the design decisions and reviewed the changes. The work before that I wrote by hand.

What I did

  • Reworked the summary PDF template, kept the classic configurator working with it, and fixed a PDF that rendered no text.
  • Built furniture-assortment features driven by the customers' Excel files: mixed assortments, disabling products per language and per market, retailer ids per language, and non-selections for every layout.
  • Added market-specific price files and price files fetched from the assortment admin.
  • Fixed an async deadlock by separating the token-refresh lock, and cut startup time for a large customer.
  • Kept every room-photo job as training data and archived unused jobs.
  • Stopped the settings log from writing secret values and patched vulnerable .NET cryptography packages.
  • Fixed tile rendering faults: tall tiles drawn turned and stretched, hexagons and triangles left unturned after a turned tile, and randomly flipped tiles forming butterfly pairs.
  • Built product groups, where the size versions of a product show as one item in the product menu, behind a setting.
  • Made a misconfigured installation explain itself, and wrote a plain-language summary for every setting.
  • Pinned the route and the image address that one customer's room display depends on in contract tests.
  • Researched running the whole showroom stack in Docker as ten question tickets, planned it as an epic of slices, and built the first one: a Linux runtime image for the backend.
