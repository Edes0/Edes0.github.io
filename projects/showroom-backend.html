Summary

The .NET backend of Spark Digital Showroom, SparkVision's room configurator. Tile, bathroom and floor retailers and manufacturers use it to show shoppers their products in a room and hand them something a shop can act on. It serves the product tree, renders configured rooms with tiles, wood floors and furniture, and produces the summary, PDF, mail and order a shopper leaves with. I worked on it from my first month to now.

What I did

  • Extended the tile and wood-floor rendering: random and column-driven wood patterns, hexagon and triangle tiles, and a new rectangle render that replaced the old one in every showroom.
  • Reworked the summary PDF template and kept the classic configurator working with it; later made the summary PDF on-demand and content-addressed, and fixed a PDF that rendered no text.
  • Built furniture-assortment features driven by the customers' Excel files: mixed assortments, disabling products per language and per market, retailer ids per language, and non-selections for every layout.
  • Added market-specific price files, price files fetched from the assortment admin, and external prices resolved by Display ID at request time.
  • Fixed an async deadlock by separating the token-refresh lock, and cut startup time for a large customer.
  • Built a mobile remote control for an in-store display, with QR-code pairing, product scanning and an inactivity timeout.
  • Planned the Bring Your Own Room epic — a shopper uploads a room photo, the surfaces are identified, and products are applied to them — and cut it into spikes and slices.
  • Built its backend: the photo-to-surface seam, the asynchronous segmentation job machinery, the photo render service, and later moved segmentation from the web host's GPU to the render node.
  • Gave a photographed room the same summary, PDF, mail, order and saved-room behaviour as a catalog room.
  • Kept every room-photo job as training data and archived unused jobs.
  • Added tile rotation at any angle and grout thickness per tile surface, carried through links, saved rooms, the summary and the order.
  • Stopped the settings log from writing secret values and patched vulnerable .NET cryptography packages.
  • Brought agent guides and Claude Code workflows into the repository and ran a team workshop on them.
  • Reviewed colleagues' pull requests across the period.

Outcomes

  • The backend serves about 95 production showroom apps, about 70 of them with outside traffic in a given month.
  • Bring Your Own Room is not launched yet.
